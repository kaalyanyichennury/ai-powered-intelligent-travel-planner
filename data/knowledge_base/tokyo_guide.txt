Title: Comprehensive Tokyo Travel & Cultural Guide
City: Tokyo, Japan
Tags: anime, temples, food, photography, electronics, culture, etiquette

Overview:
Tokyo is a mesmerizing metropolis uniting ancient heritage with futuristic innovation. Divided into distinct dynamic wards (Shibuya, Shinjuku, Asakusa, Akihabara, Ginza, Roppongi), each offers a completely unique travel atmosphere.

Key Cultural Etiquette & Local Rules:
- Tipping is NOT practiced in Japan. Leaving tips at restaurants, taxis, or hotels can cause confusion and may be politely declined.
- Public Transit: Tokyo has one of the world's most punctual railway systems (JR East and Tokyo Metro). Maintain quiet on trains; set mobile phones to silent (manner mode), and avoid phone calls. Suica or Pasmo IC cards (or digital Apple/Google Wallet cards) make transit seamless.
- Trash Disposal: Public trash cans are scarce in Japanese cities. Carry a small plastic bag in your daypack to hold personal trash until returning to your hotel or finding convenience store (konbini) bins.
- Walking and eating simultaneously (Aruki-kui) is generally discouraged in public streets, except in designated street food alleyways like Tsukiji Outer Market or Nakamise Street.

Attractions & Timings:
- Senso-ji Temple in Asakusa is Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temple. The Nakamise shopping street leading to the temple sells traditional sweets and souvenirs. The main hall is open 6:00 AM to 5:00 PM, while temple grounds are illuminated and accessible 24/7.
- Akihabara Electric Town is the global capital for anime, manga, retro video games (Super Potato, Mandarake), and maid cafes. Sunday afternoons feature pedestrian-only vehicle bans on Chuo Dori.
- Meiji Shrine (Meiji Jingu) is dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken. Entry through the colossal cypress torii gate into the forested park is free of charge.
- teamLab Planets in Toyosu offers immersive digital art installations where visitors wade through knee-deep water. Timed-entry reservations months in advance are recommended.

Photography & Cherry Blossom (Sakura) Season:
- Peak cherry blossom season typically occurs between late March and early April. Premier hanami viewing spots include Shinjuku Gyoen, Ueno Park, Meguro River, and Chidorigafuchi Moat.
- Shibuya Crossing offers superb elevated photography from the Mag's Park rooftop or the Shibuya Sky observation deck.

Budget & Dining Tips:
- Japanese convenience stores (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) sell high-quality onigiri, bento boxes, egg sandwiches, and matcha desserts for ¥150-¥600 ($1-$4 USD).
- Ticket vending machines (shokkenki) are common in ramen and noodle bars: insert cash/IC card, select your meal button, and hand the ticket to the counter chef.
